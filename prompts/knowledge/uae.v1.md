# UAE market context

Use this to understand the caller's world and to ask better questions. None of it is a fact about the caller's company. If you connect something here to their company, say it is a guess or ask.

## Where companies are with AI

- Most companies in the Gulf use AI somewhere: in a McKinsey survey of senior GCC executives, 84% said their company uses AI in at least one function, but only 31% run it at scale (Nov 2025).
- In the same survey, inaccurate output was the second most cited risk. Getting from a pilot that impresses in a demo to something that works every day is the common gap (Nov 2025).
- Many companies are at the idea or pilot stage: leadership wants an AI initiative, often prompted by national plans, but the first use case, the data and the people to build it are not settled yet.
- Arabic and English both matter. Customers write in English, Gulf Arabic, Modern Standard Arabic and Arabizi (Arabic in Latin letters), often mixed in one message.

## Industries and their usual AI problems

### Fintech and payments

- Fintech is the largest category of AI startup investment in the region (Feb 2026), and Dubai's financial free zone is growing fast: 1,933 AI, fintech and innovation firms in the first half of 2026, up 39% (Jul 2026).
- Common AI features: reading receipts and invoices, categorising spending, credit and lending decisions, and assistants that answer customers' account questions.
- What usually goes wrong: extraction that is wrong on unusual documents, assistants that answer confidently and wrongly, and no tests that catch a regression when the model or prompt changes.
- Usually owned by the CTO or head of engineering at an early-stage company.

### Property and mortgages

- Property is one of the most visible AI uses in Dubai: large portals launched conversational property search (Aug 2026), mortgage brokers run chatbots on WhatsApp (Apr 2025), and valuation tools estimate prices (Dec 2025).
- What usually goes wrong: a mortgage or eligibility chatbot that tells a buyer they qualify when they do not, search that returns poor matches, and answers that mix up Arabic and English property terms.
- Regulated advice raises the stakes: a wrong eligibility answer is a compliance problem, not only a bad experience.
- Usually owned by the CTO, the head of product, or a founder.

### Healthcare

- Clinical documentation is the active area: AI scribes that transcribe and summarise consultations, including Arabic across many dialects (Oct 2025).
- Abu Dhabi's health department has a Responsible AI Standard for AI used in health (Oct 2025).
- What usually goes wrong: transcription errors that differ by dialect, and no measurement of how often the output is wrong.
- Health data rules are strict; work happens inside the provider's own systems.

### Legal and professional services

- Tools that search and summarise contracts and regulations over a firm's own documents are early but growing (Jun 2025).
- What usually goes wrong: answers that cite the wrong clause or miss one, and no way to check answers against the source.

### Larger organisations

- Banks, government entities and energy companies mostly buy AI through large vendors and local integrators, through formal procurement (Nov 2025). They move slowly and need registered suppliers.
- Their AI teams are often in-house. A conversation with them is more likely about a specific stalled project than a new build.

## Rules and national plans

- The UAE National AI Strategy 2031 sets the national direction for AI across government and the economy (2017, still the reference).
- Dubai has directed private-sector companies toward agentic AI adoption (May 2026). Details of any funding and who qualifies have not been published.
- The UAE Central Bank issued guidance on AI for licensed financial institutions, including testing for bias and fairness every year and whenever a model changes (Feb 2026). It is guidance and applies to licensed institutions, not to every fintech.
- Abu Dhabi's Responsible AI Standard applies to AI in healthcare (Oct 2025).
- Data residency matters to many buyers. Working inside the client's own cloud account, so their data does not leave it, answers most concerns.

## How business conversations go

These are general business norms in the UAE, not rules.

- Greet before business. A short, warm greeting ("Hope you are well") before the first question is expected.
- Respectful, formal tone, especially with senior people. Use their title if they give it. Avoid slang and jokes until they set that tone.
- No pressure. Hard selling, countdowns and pushy follow-ups lose trust. Let them set the pace.
- Relationships come first. People often want to know who they are dealing with before discussing details. Be open that you are an AI agent and that a person from the team follows up.
- Decisions in family-owned businesses often rest with the owner or a senior family member, even when someone else runs the project.
- WhatsApp is the normal channel after a first contact; email is common for formal documents.
- The UAE work week is Monday to Friday. During Ramadan, working hours are shorter and meetings move later in the day.
- Many people mix English and Arabic in the same conversation.
